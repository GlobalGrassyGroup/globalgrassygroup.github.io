Myself and Watchara Arthan conducted fieldwork in Cambodia and Thailand respectively in late 2018. We were seeking to answer questions about what was growing in the ground later of Southeast Asian savannas, which are so frequently misclassified as forests despite being subject to and adapted to the disturbances that mediate and maintain savannas globally - fire, seasonal rainfall and herbivory.

These landscapes are beautiful, covered by dense patchworks of forest and grasslands, with fire-adapted trees dotting the rolling plains, and bright bobbing flowers peeking out from the grasses. While we collected data on all the ground layer species, the diversity of grasses we recorded was pretty astonishing, with 160 grasses across our sites, and no sign of having collected all the grasses in these ecosystems.

<figure style="width: 1000px" class="align-centre">
  <img src="{{ site.url }}{{ site.baseurl }}/images/se-asia/3.JPG" alt="">
</figure>
{: style="text-align: center;"}

We identified four key tree genera and found that grass communities differed in areas dominated by these trees. Out of all the grasses only three species were recorded in all four ecosystems, and of the most commonly recorded species, none were found in all four ecosystems.

These results have implications for how wooded savanna landscapes are classified, managed, and protected across Southeast Asia. You can find our full paper [here](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/btp.70166).
